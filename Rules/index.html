Rules for the Kaizo List

How KLP, PLP & the List Work

There are two separate point systems on the Kaizo List: KLP, which belongs to levels, and PLP, which belongs to players and is calculated from the KLP of everything that player has cleared. Here's the short version of how a completion turns into a player's score:

A level gets a fixed KLP value
then
Someone verifies it, or a player beats it, and earns that KLP
then
All your KLP feeds the PLP formula
then
PLP ranks you on the Player List

KLP (Kaizo List Points)

Every level on the List has a single fixed KLP value, representing how difficult it's judged (Not relative to the other levels on the list). There's no automatic formula for this number because it's decided together by the List Admins and the level's verifier and/or playtesters, weighing it against similar existing entries. Higher KLP means a harder level.

Verification vs. Victors

Verifying a level means being the one to beat it first, proving it's legitimately possible. Once a level is verified, either the verifier or the level's creator can get it uploaded to the ULB server and added to the List (this is why verifications require video proof. See the Submitting a Completion rules below).

Victoring a level means beating it after it's already been verified and added to the List. Every player who does this earns Victory KLP.

A level only has one verifier, and that role is locked in once the level is verified. The verifier can't also be counted separately as a victor on that same level. Each level only ever pays out its KLP once per player. Your Total KLP (shown on your Player Details page) is your Verification KLP plus your Victory KLP, added together across every different level you've cleared.

PLP (Player List Points)

PLP is what ranks players against each other on the Player Rankings page. Not to be confused with your KLP total. It's built to reward Kaizo players who both climb high and clear a broad, consistent spread of hard levels, rather than just stacking up volume on easy ones. It's made of three parts added together:

PLP = Peak + Consistency + Breadth

  • Peak is based on your single highest-KLP clear. That clear's KLP is raised to the power of 0.85 (a bit less than the full value, but close to it).
  • Consistency looks at every clear you have, sorted from hardest to easiest. Each one's KLP is raised to the power of 0.65, then all of those are added up and multiplied by 0.6. Your top 12 hardest clears also get an extra bonus on top of that, worth up to 60% more, which tapers down step by step until it disappears by the 12th one.
  • Breadth is a small bonus for sheer volume: take the square root of your Total KLP, then multiply it by 0.035.

Two consequences worth knowing: because Consistency uses a power less than 1, each individual clear matters a little less than the last one. Which means there are diminishing returns, but every clear still always helps some. And because your top 12 hardest clears get the extra bonus, your hardest dozen completions matter disproportionately more to your PLP than anything you clear after that.

Worked example: a player with 3 clears

Say a guy named Sasha Pupoy has verified one 500 KLP level, victored a separate 400 KLP level, and victored one more 200 KLP level.

Total KLP: 500 + 400 + 200 = 1,100 KLP

Sorted clears for PLP, hardest first: 500, 400, 200.

Peak: 500 raised to the power of 0.85 comes out to about 196.8.

Consistency: each clear's KLP raised to the power of 0.65, with the early bonus applied (500 gets the biggest bonus since it's climbed first, tapering down for 400 and 200), added up and multiplied by 0.6 - works out to about 128.4.

Breadth: the square root of 1,100 total KLP, multiplied by 0.035, comes out to about 1.2.

PLP: about 196.8 + 128.4 + 1.2 = about 326.4

Worked example: a player with 15 clears

KLP values, hardest to easiest: 900, 600, 450, 300, 250, 200, 180, 150, 140, 130, 120, 110, 100, 90, 80.

Total KLP: 3,800 KLP

Peak: about 324.4. Consistency: about 416.5. Breadth: about 2.2.

PLP: about 743.1

Note how much more total PLP this broader, more consistent player earns compared to the 3-clear example above, even though their single hardest clear (900) isn't dramatically higher than 500.

Levels & Players

1

No secret ways.

A secret way is defined as an intentional skip intended by the verifier that dramatically eases specific parts of the level, framing an otherwise illegitimate completion as valid.

2

No timmy-levels.

Levels must have coherent, kaizo-worthy gameplay. A timmy-level is one with lazy or spammy design. For example, enemies mindlessly spammed everywhere with no intentional design behind them.

3

No third-party external applications.

This includes auto-clickers, macros, or any software that automates or helps in-game inputs in any way. (Note: This includes any modifications made to the game itself, such as the ANARCHY Mod.)

Submitting a Completion

1

Provide a raw completion recording or livestream.

You must record or stream your run from start to finish. Edited, spliced footage, or providing only a screenshot is not accepted.

2

Your submission must show the Green Medal.

Both your raw completion footage and a screenshot of the level screen must clearly show the Green Medal stamp. Search the level in-game and include a clear shot of the Green Medal on the level screen as part of your submission.

3

Submit your record in the Discord.

Post your evidence in the #submit-records channel with your raw footage and Green Medal screenshot.

If any rule seems unclear, or you're unsure about something, feel free to reach out directly. New rules may be added as needed.
